# English (en) Humanize Rules

## AI tells

Vocabulary
- *delve, leverage, intricate, tapestry, landscape, realm, navigate, foster, cultivate, robust, seamless, pivotal, nuanced, holistic, paradigm, multifaceted, underscores, underscores the importance of*
- *elevate, unlock, harness, embark, journey, ever-evolving, dynamic, vibrant, rich, profound*
- *empower, streamline, facilitate, utilize, cutting-edge, transformative, game changer, paradigm shift, meticulous, paramount, beacon, supercharge*

Stock phrases
- "It's not just X, it's Y."
- "X is more than just Y; it is Z."
- "In today's fast-paced world..."
- "In the realm of..."
- "When it comes to..."
- "Plays a crucial role in..."
- "A testament to..."
- "Stands as a..."
- "Whether you're X or Y..."
- "It's worth noting / It's important to note..."
- "At its core... / In the age of... / At the end of the day..."
- "The reality is... / The truth is..."
- Throat-clearing openers: "Here's the thing," "Let me be clear," "I'll be honest," "The uncomfortable truth is."
- Faux-insight setups: "What most people get wrong...", "Here's what nobody tells you...", "The part everyone misses..."
- Importance puffery: "marks a pivotal moment," "plays a vital role," "solidifies its position."
- Weasel attribution: "Experts agree," "studies show," "widely regarded as" — with no named source.

Closing scaffolds
- "In conclusion / Overall / Ultimately / To summarize / In summary / As we've seen"
- Fake-profound kickers — a final aphorism or mic-drop metaphor. Delete, don't rewrite; end on the last concrete point.

Structural tells
- Tricolons everywhere (groups of three).
- Perfectly balanced both-sides framing on every issue.
- Every paragraph starts with a topic sentence.
- All sentences land in the 18–22 word range.
- Symmetric "X is not Y, it's Z" parallelism.
- Colon reveals: "The best part: it learns." Noun phrase + colon + dramatic lowercase reveal.
- Negative listing: "Not a X. Not a Y. A Z." Just say Z.
- Dramatic fragmentation: "That's it. That's the whole thing."
- Rhetorical setups: "What if I told you...", "Plot twist:", self-answered "Question? Answer." pairs.
- Superficial trailing analysis: "..., highlighting the team's commitment to better workflows" — trailing *-ing* clauses (highlighting, underscoring, showcasing, reflecting) that pretend to explain.
- Synonym cycling: "the agent... the assistant... the tool" for the same referent.
- Fake-strong verbs: "serves as a centralized hub for" where "is" or "tracks" is clearer.

Punctuation tells
- em-dash overuse (— in every other sentence).
- Smart quotes (" " ' ') when the surrounding text is plain.
- Consistent Oxford comma (humans drift).
- Title Case In Headings That Don't Need It.

## Human signals

Burstiness
- Sentence fragments. Like this.
- One-word sentences when emphatic.
- Long clause-stacked sentences when explaining something messy, with commas, asides, and the kind of digression that AI tends to clip out for parallelism.

Sentence openers
- Start with *And*, *But*, *So*, *Because* sometimes.
- Drop the topic sentence; start mid-thought.

Voice markers
- *honestly, tbh, kinda, sorta, I mean, look, anyway, fwiw, ngl*
- First-person hedges: *I think, I guess, feels like, in my experience* (only if the piece already has a 1st-person stance).

Parentheticals & asides
- (the kind of side note humans drop in)
- Em-dash for an aside is fine — used sparingly.

Inconsistency (controlled)
- Drop the Oxford comma in one or two places.
- ASCII dashes (-) and straight quotes (' ").
- One or two contractions even in semi-formal prose.

## Rewrite rules

| Before (AI) | After (human) |
|---|---|
| In the realm of X | In X / With X |
| It's not just X, it's Y | Y, not just X / It's Y, really |
| Plays a crucial role in | Matters for / Drives / Runs |
| In today's fast-paced world | (delete) |
| Whether you're a beginner or an expert | (delete or pick one audience) |
| A tapestry of X | A mix of X / lots of X |
| Navigate the complexities of | Deal with / Figure out |
| Leverage X to Y | Use X for Y / Y with X |
| Delve into | Get into / Look at |
| Foster X | Build X / Grow X |
| Robust, scalable solution | Solid solution / Works at scale |
| Underscores the importance of | Shows X matters / Highlights X |
| In conclusion, X is important | (delete prefix; state X) |
| Furthermore / Moreover / Additionally | Also / And / Plus |
| It is worth noting that | (delete) |
| One must consider | You have to think about |
| — overused em-dash | comma, period, or parens |
| Title Case Headings | Sentence case headings |
| The question isn't X, it's Y | Y matters more than X |
| The best part: it learns | It learns, which is the point (plain sentence) |
| ..., highlighting its commitment to X | ..., so [the concrete consequence] |
| serves as a hub for | is / tracks / holds |
| made a decision / has the ability to | decided / can |
| Experts agree / studies show | name the source, or cut the claim |
| marks a pivotal moment for | (state the plain fact: "is the company's first paid product") |
| final mic-drop metaphor | (delete; end on the last concrete point) |

Vocabulary swap defaults (use sparingly — don't overwrite domain terms):
- *utilize* → *use*
- *commence* → *start*
- *terminate* → *end / stop*
- *demonstrate* → *show*
- *facilitate* → *help / let*
- *implement* → *build / ship / do*
- *individual(s)* → *person / people*

## Examples

### Example 1 — Generic blog opener

Before:
> In today's fast-paced digital landscape, artificial intelligence has emerged as a pivotal force that is reshaping industries. Whether you're a seasoned professional or just starting your journey, understanding AI is no longer optional — it's essential.

After:
> AI is rewiring most industries now. If you work in tech and you're not at least loosely tracking it, you're going to fall behind. That's about it.

### Example 2 — Stacked tells

Before:
> Leveraging cutting-edge methodologies, our framework facilitates the seamless integration of disparate data sources, fostering a holistic understanding that empowers stakeholders to make data-driven decisions.

After:
> Our framework pulls together data from different sources so the people using it can actually make calls based on numbers, not vibes.

### Example 3 — Tricolon + em-dash overload

Before:
> The product is fast, reliable, and intuitive — designed for power users, casual users, and everyone in between.

After:
> It's fast and it doesn't break. Power users get what they need, and casual users don't get lost.

## Notes

- Don't strip domain terminology. *Kubernetes, eigenvector, mitochondria* stay.
- Don't invent first-person voice if the original is third-person institutional.
- Don't add typos. One or two relaxed punctuation choices are fine; misspellings are not.
- If the clear word is right, repeat it — don't rotate synonyms for style.
- Keep "just," "honestly," "I think," "maybe" when they carry real uncertainty, emphasis, or the writer's spoken rhythm. Cut them only when they add nothing.
- Protect the specific fact: "significantly improves productivity" → keep or recover the concrete number ("cut review time from 30 minutes to 8"), never smooth a detail into generic importance.
